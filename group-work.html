THINKING

GROWING TOGETHER.

Individual ability creates potential. Collaboration gives that potential a shared direction. This page explores the principles of teamwork, the value of different perspectives and the lessons that can emerge from working towards a common objective.

01
COLLECTIVE THINKING

01 / THE EXPERIENCE

BEYOND INDIVIDUAL WORK

Different perspectives.
Shared responsibility.

Group work introduces an important dimension to academic development: the ability to work with others towards an outcome that depends on more than individual effort. It requires people to combine their knowledge, recognise different strengths and contribute towards a common purpose.

Effective collaboration involves more than dividing an assignment into smaller tasks. It also requires listening carefully, exchanging ideas, managing responsibilities and remaining accountable to the people who depend on your contribution.

I see teamwork as an opportunity to develop skills that extend beyond academic assignments. The ability to communicate clearly, consider alternative viewpoints and coordinate individual efforts is equally relevant in professional environments, particularly where decisions and projects involve people with different areas of expertise.

02 / TEAM DEVELOPMENT

HOW A TEAM
TAKES SHAPE.

Effective collaboration develops over time. Bruce Tuckman's model of group development describes five stages that can help explain how teams establish relationships, navigate disagreements, coordinate their work and reflect on their outcomes.

These stages provide a useful framework for understanding teamwork, although real teams may revisit earlier stages as their circumstances or responsibilities change.

01
STAGE ONE

Forming

Forming is the initial stage in which team members become familiar with one another, interpret the assignment and begin identifying what needs to be accomplished.

At this stage, I recognise the importance of understanding the task before beginning execution. Establishing a shared objective, clarifying expectations and respecting different viewpoints can give a team a stronger starting point.

FOUNDATION
02
STAGE TWO

Storming

During storming, differences in opinions, personalities and working styles may create tension. Members might disagree about priorities, approaches or the distribution of responsibilities.

This stage demonstrates why disagreement should be approached constructively rather than treated as an automatic sign of failure. Patience, respectful discussion and a willingness to reconsider one's position can help a team move towards a workable solution.

ADJUSTMENT
03
STAGE THREE

Norming

During norming, members increasingly understand their responsibilities and establish more effective ways of working. Communication becomes more organised, and the team begins to develop shared expectations.

I see this stage as a reminder that cooperation depends on more than good intentions. Members need to understand their roles, respect agreed deadlines and recognise how individual contributions affect the overall quality of the work.

ALIGNMENT
04
STAGE FOUR

Performing

During performing, a team can work more effectively towards its objectives because members understand their responsibilities and coordinate their efforts. Attention shifts towards solving problems and delivering meaningful results.

This stage illustrates the value of preparation, communication and trust. When members contribute reliably and remain focused on the shared objective, the team is better positioned to turn individual effort into collective progress.

EXECUTION
05
STAGE FIVE

Adjourning

Adjourning describes the conclusion of a team's work. Once an assignment or project is complete, members can review the outcome, acknowledge their efforts and consider what they learned.

Reflection is important because completing a task does not automatically mean that every part of the process was effective. Reviewing communication, time management and task coordination can reveal what should be maintained or improved in future collaborations.

REFLECTION

03 / INDIVIDUAL PERSPECTIVE

MY PLACE
IN THE TEAM.

I view contribution to group work as more than completing an assigned responsibility. It also means remaining engaged with the shared objective, contributing considered ideas and being receptive to the knowledge and perspectives of other members.

My interest in analytical subjects encourages me to approach problems thoughtfully and examine information before reaching conclusions. I also value clear communication because an idea becomes more useful when it can be explained in a way that others understand and can respond to.

At the same time, effective teamwork requires recognising that personal strengths do not remove the need to listen, adapt or accept feedback. Collaboration is not about ensuring that one person's approach always prevails; it is about identifying approaches that help the team accomplish its purpose.

Going forward, I want to contribute by clarifying expectations early, communicating consistently and ensuring that my work supports the progress of the entire team. I also recognise the importance of raising challenges early instead of allowing small misunderstandings to affect the final result.

04 / WHAT THE PROCESS TEACHES

LESSONS THAT
EXTEND BEYOND
THE ASSIGNMENT.

01

Communication

Clear communication helps members understand expectations, exchange ideas and prevent avoidable misunderstandings. It also creates opportunities to address concerns before they affect the group's progress.

02

Accountability

Shared objectives require individuals to take ownership of their responsibilities. Completing tasks on time and communicating delays helps protect the progress of others.

03

Adaptability

Working with different perspectives requires openness to alternative approaches. Adaptability helps a team respond to new information, changing circumstances and constructive feedback.

04

Collective Progress

A strong collective outcome depends on contributions that work together. Individual effort becomes more valuable when it advances the group's shared purpose rather than remaining disconnected from it.

05 / PERSONAL REFLECTION

THE RESULT IS
IMPORTANT.
THE PROCESS
IS EQUALLY SO.

Reflecting on teamwork has helped me recognise that placing people in a group does not automatically produce effective collaboration. A successful process depends on how members communicate, distribute responsibilities, manage differences and remain committed to a common objective.

One important lesson is that individual responsibility and collective responsibility must operate together. A member may complete an assigned task successfully, but the wider assignment can still suffer if that work is delivered late, communicated poorly or fails to connect with the contributions of others. This highlights the importance of coordination alongside individual effort.

In future group assignments, I would place greater emphasis on agreeing on responsibilities and deadlines at the beginning, maintaining regular communication and reviewing progress before the final submission. I would also encourage team members to raise difficulties early so that the group has time to respond rather than relying on last-minute solutions.

I also want to become more intentional about seeking feedback. It is easy to focus on whether my own contribution is complete, but a more useful question is whether that contribution helps the entire team produce its best work. This shift in perspective encourages me to evaluate both the quality of my individual effort and its impact on the shared outcome.

Ultimately, teamwork requires a balance between independence and cooperation. I must be capable of taking responsibility for my own work while remaining willing to listen, adjust and support the efforts of others. These are qualities I intend to continue developing throughout my academic and professional journey.
